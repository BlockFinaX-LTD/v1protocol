BLOCKFINAX PROTOCOL
Operational and Infrastructure Overview
Prepared for internal compliance orientation. All addresses verified on chain.


1. WHAT BLOCKFINAX DOES

BlockFinaX operates an on chain marketplace for hedging foreign exchange risk in emerging
markets. Individuals and businesses exposed to a weakening local currency can buy parametric
protection that pays out automatically based on an exchange rate reported by oracles. The
counterparties who fund those payouts are liquidity providers, also called underwriters, who
deposit stablecoin capital and earn premiums in return.

The company does not take a market position. It operates the software and earns service fees
on activity. Payouts are funded entirely by liquidity provider capital, not by the company.


2. CUSTODY MODEL

The protocol is non custodial. User funds are never held in a company bank account or a company
controlled omnibus wallet. When a user deposits, buys protection, or receives a payout, the funds
move directly into and out of audited smart contracts on public blockchains. The company cannot
freeze, reassign, or spend user principal through the normal contract interface. The company can
withdraw only the accrued service fees, and only up to the fee amount recorded by the contract.

All value is denominated in regulated stablecoins. The contracts do not accept or hold native
cryptocurrency such as ETH.


3. NETWORKS AND SMART CONTRACTS

The protocol is deployed on two public networks. Each network runs one upgradeable smart contract
system built on the EIP 2535 Diamond standard. The main contract address is stable and never

4. HOW A TRANSACTION FLOWS

Step 1. A creator opens a hedge event for a currency pair, for example USD to Ghana Cedi, and
        deposits initial liquidity.
Step 2. Liquidity providers deposit stablecoins and receive proportional pool shares.
Step 3. A hedger buys protection. They pay a premium, which is distributed to the liquidity
        providers, plus a service fee. The maximum possible payout is reserved from pool capital
        at this moment so the pool always holds enough to cover it.
Step 4. At the scheduled expiry date, oracles report the settlement exchange rate.
Step 5. If the protection triggered, the hedger claims a payout. Liquidity providers reclaim their
        capital net of any amount paid out. Premiums can be claimed at any time.

Settlement is European style. An event can only be settled at or after its expiry date. The
outcome is decided by the rate at expiry.


5. SERVICE FEES AND REVENUE

The company earns four fee types plus a loyalty share. All are collected in the same stablecoin as
the event.

Event creation fee. A flat fee charged when a creator opens an event. Currently set to 2 USD,
  scheduled to be raised to 25 USD.
Hedger fee. A percentage charged when protection is bought. Currently 0.5 percent of notional. A
  planned change will rebase this to 2.5 percent of the premium.
Payout fee. 1 percent of a payout, deducted when a hedger claims.
Liquidity provider fee. 1 percent of premium income, deducted when a provider claims premiums.
Creator loyalty. 5 percent of every service fee is redirected to the event creator as an incentive.

Fees accrue inside the contract and are withdrawn by the operator to a company wallet. As of this
overview the accrued protocol revenue is small, consistent with an early stage pilot.

7. OFF CHAIN SYSTEMS

Pricing engine. Calculates fair premium quotes for each event and signs them with the pricing
  engine signer key listed above. This keeps pricing consistent and verifiable on chain.

Transaction service. A backend service that helps users submit transactions. It uses account
  abstraction, an industry standard where a user operates through a smart contract wallet and a
  third party paymaster can sponsor network fees. The paymaster contract observed in use is
  0x777777777777AeC03fd955926DbF81597e66834C. Users retain control of their own funds and approvals.

Application. The consumer facing website and backend live in a separate code repository. The on
  chain contracts do not perform identity checks. Any user level onboarding, screening, or
  eligibility controls are handled at the application layer, outside the smart contracts.


8. GOVERNANCE AND KEY MANAGEMENT

Current state. On both networks the contracts are owned by the single operator wallet listed in
  section 6, and software updates apply immediately. That same wallet is also the current
  settlement submitter. The 2 of 3 governance Safes are deployed but ownership has not yet been
  transferred to them, and the time delay module is not yet installed.

Planned hardening. The roadmap moves control to the 2 of 3 Safes and adds a 48 hour delay on any
  software update, so that changes require multiple approvers and a public waiting period. Tooling
  for this transition is prepared. Separating the settlement key from the ownership key is also
  planned.


9. SECURITY POSTURE

The smart contract code has undergone an internal engineering review and an automated test suite
with full function coverage on the production contracts. A formal independent third party audit
has not yet been performed and is recommended before scaling total value locked.

Core protections in the contracts include the following. Payouts are always fully collateralized
by liquidity provider capital reserved at purchase time. Reentrancy protection and a checks then
effects then interactions pattern guard all fund movements. Internal accounting tracks balances
rather than trusting raw token balances, which prevents balance manipulation. The operator is
blocked from sweeping the payment token, so user principal cannot be withdrawn through the rescue
function.


10. SUMMARY

BlockFinaX runs non custodial foreign exchange hedging software on the Base and BNB Smart Chain
networks, denominated in USDC and USDT. Users retain custody of their funds inside audited smart
contracts. The company earns service fees on activity and does not take market risk. Administrative
control currently rests with a single operator key, with a transition to multisignature governance
and a settlement key separation planned. Off chain systems provide pricing, fee sponsored
transaction submission, and the consumer application, none of which take custody of user principal.
